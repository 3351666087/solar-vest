30 W source, 5 W sink: current limiting is mandatory A 30 W input at 90% efficiency can push 6.9 A into a 3.9 V battery, while the safe charge rate of a 2500 mAh cell is 0.5 C = 1.25 A. Without Rcs limiting, the battery will be overcharged to failure.
Irradiance simulation: only uniform whole-panel attenuation Use a neutral-density mesh across the entire panel. Never shade partially — a shaded cell is reverse-biased into a hot spot (can exceed 100 °C, damaging the panel or worse), and the I-V curve develops multiple peaks that lock the MPPT onto the wrong one.

Circulating current: ΔV = 0.5 V, ~50 mΩ cell ESR each ⚠, ~20 mΩ wiring → I = 0.5 ÷ (0.05 + 0.05 + 0.02) ≈ 4.2 A, far beyond the safe discharge rate of a 2500 mAh cell — repeatedly tripping the PCM and damaging the cells.

TC-13 — mandatory verification before assembly Use adjustable current-limited supplies throughout; never real batteries. Supply 1 → BAT_A at 3.90 V / 100 mA limit; supply 2 → BAT_B at 3.70 V / 100 mA limit (a 0.2 V difference is enough to expose any parallel path). ① Sweep all 16 gate combinations — any path > 10 mA is a FAIL; ② with all FETs off, current should be ~0 (else a body diode is backwards); ③ fault injection: force Q1=Q2=ON, the interlock must block it; ④ on MCU reset or power loss all FETs default OFF.
Real batteries only after all checks pass.
Battery temperature window NTCs on the cell surface (thermal grease + Kapton). No charging below 0 °C or above 45 °C — low-temperature charging plates lithium, a common cause of safety incidents. ⚠ The CN3791 has no built-in temperature protection; this must be done in firmware.

Easiest mistake: P-MOS body-diode orientation If Q3/Q4 are backwards, the body diodes keep both batteries paralleled even with the gates off. Prefer an ideal-diode IC with reverse blocking (e.g. LM66100) — far more reliable than back-to-back discrete FETs.
At schematic review, draw each body diode explicitly; after assembly verify with a DMM: with Q3 off, BAT_A+ ↔ LOAD_BUS must be open in both directions (conduction in either direction = wrong orientation; rework).
Break-away: a cable crossing the body Falls and dressing will yank it. A magnetic or frangible connector is mandatory (> ~20 N releases the connector rather than tugging electrodes); leave slack and clip the cable; on disconnect the supercap holds power → MCU logs the event → orderly shutdown.

Pre-charge: no direct paralleling Slapping a discharged 5 F cap onto a 4.0 V lithium cell pulls tens of amps instantaneously. Pre-charge is mandatory: 10 Ω current limit → Q6 bypass once ΔV < 50 mV. Confirm the cap is discharged (< 0.1 V) before soldering; supercaps are polarized — reverse them and they bulge.
π filter and sync injection The 10 µH + 22 µF ×2 π filter is the second EMC line of defense (the first is architectural isolation). The sync pulse reaches I1 through 1 kΩ + BAT54S clamp; ⚠ verify level tolerance, and the two ends must share ground.
Two non-negotiable hardware constraints　 ① Kelvin 4-wire — sense taps at the pad center, never sharing the current path; else 10–30% systematic error corrupts EAR. ② Physical partitioning — ≥ 3 mm copper-free band between 18 V and LV zones; no shared harness or connector.
