SECTION 04 · FIRMWARE

Firmware Layer — Scheduling, Timestamps & Safety

The telemetry MCU does exactly three things: metering, scheduling, synchronization. It does not acquire biosignals (that is BITalino's job) and does no signal processing (that is the host's job) — this purity exists for verifiability: if the telemetry MCU also acquired, it could not independently verify the acquisition chain.

The 4 invariants of battery_switch Every line of this module is hardware-safety relevant and needs full unit tests. Runtime asserts:
① Q1 and Q2 never on together; ② Q3 and Q4 never on together; ③ the same battery never charges and discharges at once; ④ at least one battery always discharges (else the load loses power).
Switching is break-before-make with 1 ms dead time, the gap bridged by the supercap; every switch is logged as an event — post-processing must check for a matching artifact on the ECG.

4.2　Firmware modules and the test boundary

Coulomb counting: why no dedicated fuel-gauge chip SoC comes from bidirectional current integration on the battery-side INA226 — MAX17048 is gone. Three reasons:
① Package — MAX17048 is TDFN/WLP bottom-pad; the lab's tools can't do it;
② Scientific credibility — ModelGauge is a black box; raw coulomb counting drifts but is explainable;
③ Resolution — 16 bit + 0.02 Ω → 125 µA LSB, better than voltage-based estimation.
Cost　power-loss persistence (flash write/60 s), full/empty recalibration, temperature correction, plus one full charge–discharge cycle at start to learn true capacity (nominal 2500 mAh often measures 2200–2400 mAh — uncalibrated SoC runs high).
Two-ended sync pulses: a small but critical design A 10 ms sync pulse is emitted at each end of every acquisition window. Between the two pulses, the MCU-side elapsed time is known (µs) and the BITalino-side sample count is known — their ratio is the true rate of the BITalino sampling clock.
It will not be exactly 1000.000 Hz but 999.x or 1000.x Hz — uncorrected, hundreds of ms of drift in 10 minutes; the most commonly ignored error source in long recordings.
Every run report states the measured drift ppm; if it moves with temperature across runs, that itself is a finding worth publishing.
Offline log: disconnection is the norm, not the exception BLE drops are inevitable on-body. While disconnected, samples go to flash; on reconnect the backlog replays before live streaming, deduplicated by seq. 20 B/sample × 0.1 Hz ≈ 173 KB/day — the nRF52840's 1 MB flash holds ~5 days; for longer add a W25Q128 (16 MB ≈ 90 days).
