SECTION 04 · FIRMWARE

Firmware Layer — Scheduling, Timestamps & Safety

The telemetry MCU does exactly three things: metering, scheduling, synchronization. It does not acquire biosignals (that is BITalino's job) and does no signal processing (that is the host's job) — keeping its duties this narrow is purely for verifiability: if the telemetry MCU also took part in acquisition, it could not be used to independently verify the acquisition chain.

The 4 invariants of battery_switch Every line of this module is hardware-safety relevant and needs full unit tests. Runtime asserts:
① Q1 and Q2 never on together; ② Q3 and Q4 never on together; ③ the same battery never charges and discharges at once; ④ at least one battery always discharges (else the load loses power).
Switching is break-before-make with 1 ms dead time, the gap bridged by the supercap; every switch is logged as an event — post-processing must check for a matching artifact on the ECG.

4.2 Firmware modules and the test boundary

Coulomb counting: why no dedicated fuel-gauge chip SoC comes from bidirectional current integration on the battery-side INA226 — MAX17048 is gone. Three reasons:
① Package — MAX17048 is a TDFN/WLP bottom-pad part that neither the lab equipment nor a soldering iron can handle;
② Scientific credibility — ModelGauge is a black-box algorithm; you cannot know what corrections it applies internally. Raw coulomb integration drifts, but it is explainable and can be stated clearly in the paper;
③ Resolution — 16 bit + 0.02 Ω → 125 µA LSB, better than voltage-based estimation.
Cost it must handle power-loss persistence (flash write every 60 s), recalibration on full-charge events (CV-phase current falls to 0.05 C), recalibration on deep discharge, and temperature correction, and one complete full-charge/full-discharge cycle must be run at project start to calibrate the true capacity (a cell rated 2500 mAh may measure only 2200–2400 mAh; without calibration SoC reads systematically high).
Two-ended sync pulses: a small but critical design A 10 ms sync pulse is emitted at each end of every acquisition window. Between the two pulses, the MCU-side elapsed time is known (µs) and the BITalino-side sample count is known — their ratio is the true rate of the BITalino sampling clock.
You will find it is not exactly 1000.000 Hz but 999.x or 1000.x Hz. Left uncorrected, this drifts by several hundred milliseconds within 10 minutes — the most commonly overlooked error source in long-term recordings.
Every experiment report must state the measured drift in ppm; if it varies with temperature between runs, that in itself is a finding worth writing into the paper.
Offline log: disconnection is the norm, not the exception In on-body experiments BLE disconnections are certain to happen (walking out of range, phone screen locking, interference). While disconnected, telemetry samples are written to flash; after reconnecting, the backlog is uploaded first and then the device switches to the live stream, with the host deduplicating by seq. 20 B/sample × 0.1 Hz ≈ 173 KB/day — the nRF52840's internal 1 MB flash holds about 5 days; for longer, add an external W25Q128 (16 MB ≈ 90 days).
