SECTION 02

System Architecture

Four layers. Hardware solves energy and noise; firmware solves scheduling and timestamps; software solves multi-source alignment and analysis; the data layer solves reproducibility.

2.1　Two decisions that shape the architecture

ADR-007　Split topology: no 18 V on the chest The system splits into two physical units. The 18 V power chain, MPPT charger, and batteries all stay in the backpack unit; only < 4.2 V reaches the chest unit over a 5-core shielded cable. Four problems solved at once:
① EMC — noise sources sit 0.5–1 m from the ECG amplifier with no common-mode path between; ② Safety — 18 V physically cannot approach the ECG electrodes; ③ Heat — all charger heat stays in the backpack; chest-unit dissipation < 1 mW; ④ Ergonomics — panel and battery weight rides in the backpack; the chest carries only tens of grams.
Cost　one extra cable → a break-away design is mandatory (magnetic or frangible connector).
ADR-006　Dual-battery alternation: solve EMC by physical isolation, not filtering Two batteries: at any instant one is charging, the other is powering the load, roles swapped by the MCU every 30 min. During acquisition no switching supply is operating on the supply rail at all — noise is not filtered out, it simply does not exist in the measurement loop. The few-ms switchover gap is bridged by a supercapacitor: 5.4 V / 5 F stores 15.2 J, holding a 240 mW load for 63 s — a margin of over 10,000×.
Precondition　a hardware interlock is mandatory to prevent accidental parallel connection of the batteries (a 0.5 V difference drives ~4.2 A of circulating current). This circuit must not see real batteries until TC-13 fully passes.
Deploy both together? Measure first, then decide the complexity ADR-006 is not cheap. Recommendation: in Phase 1 implement only the software-level "charger off during acquisition" (ADR-004), and quantify the noise with the TC-05 ECG-phantom comparison:
· if the noise-floor delta < 10 µVrms① → drop the second battery, halve hardware complexity;
· if above → build ADR-006 and re-measure.
Do not invest hardware effort in dual batteries before TC-05 returns a result.

2.2　Layer responsibilities and boundaries

L1Hardware layer — energy & noise
Harvest, convert, store, and distribute energy without polluting the biosignal. Key constraints: every power node must be meterable (a precondition for EAR); the 18 V zone and the low-voltage zone must be physically partitioned.
L2Firmware layer — scheduling & timestamps
Energy state machine, battery switching, coulomb counting, hardware sync pulses, BLE telemetry and offline logging. Key constraints: the state machine must contain no hardware calls so it can be unit-tested on a PC; on watchdog failure the load must default to OFF.
L3Software layer — alignment & analysis
Capture, time-align, assess signal quality, compute energy metrics, and visualize across three independent data sources. Key constraint: time-alignment error must be < 10 ms and provable (not assumed).
L4Data & ops layer — reproducibility
Raw data immutable, processing re-runnable, every run traceable to the firmware and hardware versions of its day. Do not skimp on this layer — when an anomalous curve shows up three months from now, it is the only thing that can save you.
① Engineering rule-of-thumb threshold; the formal report should cite the IEC 60601-2-25 quantified noise requirements for ECG equipment.
