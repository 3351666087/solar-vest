SolarVest Wiring Guide

For bench bring-up: without wearing the vest, connect the back pod, chest pod, C and A together on the desk. Every wire is specified down to the pin. Socket positions and pin numbers on PMB-H and LIB are taken from the two boards’ KiCad files, positions inside the enclosures from the printed-part source code, and wire colours from the wiring table. Click any wire in the diagram and the panel on the right shows how to connect it.

1Three polarised connections have no reverse-polarity protection: battery into J2, W07 into Core B, W12 into C. Before plugging in, confirm polarity using the method on the wire’s card.
2The W06 5-core cable must be pin 1 to pin 1. Check it once with the continuity (beep) range before powering up; a reversed cable shorts the load output straight to ground.
3While electrodes are on a person, do not plug USB into the XIAO. With the Mac on its charger, the body would be connected to mains earth through USB.

Overview

The system has 4 units and 14 wire groups (the W08 sync wire has been dropped in favour of software synchronisation). This diagram only shows what connects to what; block positions are schematic. For the real socket positions on the boards, see the three millimetre-scale drawings that follow.

Chest pod: LIB and Core B

Top view with the lid removed. LIB is rotated 180° in the pod, so its silkscreen reads upside down, and the drawing shows it upside down as well. Core B’s port positions match the lid engraving: right side, bottom to top, I1 / A4 / A3 / A2 / A1. It is fitted correctly when the switch and micro-USB are at the end away from LIB, next to the O1–A5 row (PLUX manual Fig. 12).

Core B’s battery socket is at the PWR end (the white 2P socket next to the switch and micro-USB), the farthest point from LIB, so W07 has to cross over the top of the Core. The socket position in the drawing is estimated from your photo, accurate to a few millimetres.

C and A

C is the Board Kit with its own battery, serving as the control for B. A is an unmodified stock HeartBIT with only the PulseSensor connected. Neither goes through PMB-H.

C (Board Kit)

W12523450 battery → battery socket on the Board power block. Red = +. Check polarity once per the W12 card before plugging in
W113-lead electrode cable → left-hand ECG port (lid engraved ECG ←). Red C_IN+, black C_IN−, white C_REF

Temperature (W14 / W15)

The plugs of the two type-K thermocouples go only into the thermometer, never into any circuit board. TC1 is taped with aluminium-foil tape to the centre of the panel backsheet; TC2 goes on the vest surface under the panel (during bench work, on the desk or on clothing).

Wire by wire

Wires marked “Bench: required” are connected for this bring-up; “optional” wires can be left off and bring-up still completes; “On-body only” wires are connected once the vest is made.

Bench bring-up: 11 steps

Work in order; move on only when the readings of each step are correct. Ticks are stored only in this device’s browser. The green text on the right is the expected reading. You need: multimeter, breadboard plus 1 PH2.0 straight-pin header, soldering station and heat-shrink, a Mac with Arduino IDE installed, an Android phone, disposable ECG electrodes.

    Test sketch

    Used in step 5. It is for bring-up only: it scans the 6 INA226s, prints voltage, current and NTC temperature every second, and lets you toggle the load, panel, charging and sync pulse with serial commands. The load is off at power-up, matching the board’s hardware default. This is not the production firmware: no BLE and no thermal-protection logic.
